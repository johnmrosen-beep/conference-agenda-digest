# Notes on the October 2026 model comparison

Report: [2026-10-22T06-03-09.md](2026-10-22T06-03-09.md) (Haiku stage `2026-10-21T18-48-35` plus Sonnet stage).
Data: 62 presentations on 14 pages of the NASPGHAN 2026 program book, in 5 golden slices, each
checked against the PDF by a pediatric gastroenterologist. Each model and effort level ran 3 times.

## Decision

**Claude Haiku 5.5, medium effort.** The rule fixed before any results ("use Haiku unless Sonnet
beats it by at least 5 points of presenter or institution F1, or 3 points of presentation recall")
was not met: Sonnet's lead was 0.0, 0.4 and 0.0 points. Sonnet costs about 13 times as much per
pass on these pages ($0.53 vs $0.04), and about 20 times as much for a whole program book.

## What else the numbers show

- **Both models are near ceiling** on finding presentations, presenters and authors (F1 1.00).
- **Start times are Haiku's weak spot**: 0.90-0.92 accuracy vs Sonnet's 0.98. The rule did not
  include times, and it is not being changed after the fact; instead this becomes the next fix
  (find which times Haiku misses and whether code can recover them from the page text, as it
  already does for abstract numbers).
- **Session assignment is Sonnet's weak spot**: 0.80-0.86 accuracy vs Haiku's 0.97-1.00.
- **Repeats agree closely**; the widest spread is Sonnet's session scores (0.63-0.94 at medium).
- **Effort barely matters** for either model on these pages. Haiku at medium was about 30% slower
  than at low for the same cost.
- **The cost estimate is cautious**: real runs cost about a third of the high estimate.

## Changes to the answer key after seeing results

Recorded here because changing the answer key after seeing scores is the kind of thing an honest
evaluation has to disclose. Both changes corrected the key itself, not a model's score:

1. **Added poster 654** (posters-sat). Every Sonnet run reported it; the drafting parser had missed
   it because an award name is printed between its number and its title. Human review had not
   caught the gap either. The evaluation checked the answer key as well as the models.
2. **Removed the plenary's "Introduction and Welcome"** (plenary-cs1), by reviewer decision: a
   welcome is not something anyone adds to a personal agenda. Both models already left it out.

Also during review, before any runs: Rady Children's Health and CHOC (now merged) are counted as
one institution, while UC San Diego and UC Irvine remain separate.

## Follow-up: closing Haiku's start-time gap

Report: [2026-10-22T06-57-02.md](2026-10-22T06-57-02.md) (Haiku only, after the fixes below; $0.23).

1. **Diagnosis.** A per-field error list showed every wrong time was the session's start time on
   the Research Abstracts pages, where each talk's time is printed on its own line above it.
2. **First fix (code, measured for free by re-merging saved answers):** recover a time printed
   just above a talk's number. Start-time accuracy rose from 0.90/0.92 to 0.97/0.98.
3. **Root cause (found with `eval:why`):** the PDF stores page 75's bottom half before its top
   half, so the text Claude read was out of order. The text is now put in reading order (top to
   bottom, left to right) before anything uses it.
4. **Result:** Haiku at medium effort scored 1.00 on start time, date, end time and room, closing
   the one gap where Sonnet had been ahead (0.98). Sonnet was not re-run; the decision stands.

Remaining: in 1 of 3 low-effort runs the outline treated an ALL-CAPS topic heading inside a poster
session as a session of its own (6 posters lost their time and room); the outline prompt now says
topic headings are themes, not sessions. Medium effort, the app's setting, never did this.

## Follow-up: merging institution names (milestone 7)

Reports: [2026-10-22T18-39-18.md](2026-10-22T18-39-18.md) (first try) and
[2026-10-22T18-39-18-remerged.md](2026-10-22T18-39-18-remerged.md) (fixed naming, replayed for free).

| Haiku, medium effort | Institutions F1 | Distinct institutions F1 |
|---|---|---|
| No merging (previous run) | 0.98 | 0.98 |
| Merging, Claude names each group | 0.89 | 0.87 |
| Merging, group named with a printed spelling | **0.99** | **0.99** |

One extra Claude call groups the distinct institution names. The first version also asked Claude
for each group's "full official name"; those new names appear nowhere in the program, so they
could not be matched, and accuracy fell. Claude now only decides which names belong together and
code names the group with a printed spelling. Replaying the saved grouping under the new rule
(no new API calls) showed the grouping itself was sound: no different places were merged.

Remaining mismatches are extraction detail rather than merging: Claude occasionally reports two
institutions as one name ("Munroe Meyer Institute, University of Nebraska Medical Center"). The
answer key was not changed to accept these.
